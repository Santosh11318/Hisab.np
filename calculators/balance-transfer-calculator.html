Formula used

Net savings = Interest cost at current rate − Interest cost at new rate − Transfer fee

How it works

Transferring your loan balance to a bank offering a lower interest rate can reduce total interest paid over the remaining tenure — but the one-time transfer fee eats into those savings, so it's worth comparing net, not just the rate difference.

Why it helps

  • Puts a rupee figure on 'is switching banks worth it'.
  • Accounts for the transfer fee, not just the headline rate cut.
  • Useful leverage when negotiating a rate with your current bank too.

Worked example

Transferring a Rs 25 lakh balance from 11% to 9% over a 15-year remaining tenure typically saves several lakh rupees even after a 1% transfer fee.

FAQs

Many Nepali banks will renegotiate your rate if you show a competing offer, which can save the hassle and fee of an actual transfer.

Some banks also charge legal, valuation, or documentation fees for a balance transfer — factor those in alongside the percentage fee shown here.
