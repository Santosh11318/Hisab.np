Formula used

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / [(1 + r)n − 1]

Where P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and n is the total number of monthly instalments.

How it works

Every EMI is split into an interest portion and a principal portion. Early in the loan, most of your EMI goes toward interest because the outstanding balance is high; as the balance shrinks, more of each EMI goes toward principal. The amortization schedule above shows this shift year by year.

Why it helps

  • Plan your monthly budget before applying for a loan.
  • See exactly how much interest you'll pay over the loan's life.
  • Test whether a small prepayment meaningfully shortens your loan.
  • Compare EMIs across loan types before choosing a bank.

Worked example

A Rs 30 lakh home loan at 9.5% for 20 years works out to an EMI of roughly Rs 27,950, with total interest of about Rs 37 lakh paid over the tenure — adjust the sliders above to match your own loan offer.

FAQs

Most Nepali banks apply extra payments to reduce the loan tenure while keeping the EMI the same — that's what this calculator assumes for the "compare scenario" option.

The calculator assumes a fixed rate for simplicity. If your bank uses a floating rate, your actual EMI or tenure will vary as the base rate changes.

Banks in Nepal typically look at income proof, existing liabilities, collateral value and credit history — this calculator only estimates the EMI, not your eligibility.
