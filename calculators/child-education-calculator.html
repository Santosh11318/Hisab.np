Formula used

Future Cost = Current Cost × (1 + education inflation)ⁿ; Required SIP = Future Cost ÷ SIP growth factor.

How it works

Education costs in Nepal often rise faster than general inflation, so this inflates today's cost by an education-specific rate before calculating the monthly SIP needed to fund it by the target year.

Why it helps

  • Accounts for education-specific inflation, which is usually higher than general CPI.
  • Turns a distant, large goal into an actionable monthly number.
  • Encourages starting early, when the required SIP is smallest.

Worked example

An education costing Rs 20 lakh today, needed in 12 years at 9% education inflation, would cost roughly Rs 56 lakh then — needing about Rs 21,000-22,000/month at an 11% return.

FAQs

Rising demand for quality education, foreign university costs, and specialized program fees tend to push education costs up faster than the general cost of living.
