Formula used

A = P × (1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ, where n is the compounding frequency per year and t is the number of years.

How it works

Compound interest earns 'interest on interest' — each compounding period adds interest to the principal, and the next period's interest is calculated on that larger base.

Why it helps

  • Shows exactly how compounding frequency (yearly vs monthly) affects the same nominal rate.
  • Useful for comparing fixed deposits with different compounding terms.
  • A foundational calculation behind almost every other calculator on this site.

Worked example

Rs 2,00,000 at 9% compounded quarterly for 5 years grows to roughly Rs 3.11 lakh — slightly more than annual compounding at the same nominal rate.

FAQs

Yes, for the same nominal annual rate, more frequent compounding always produces a slightly higher final amount.

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal every period; compound interest is calculated on the principal plus all previously earned interest.
